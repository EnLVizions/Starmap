Star map v5: notes

New in v5 - Star data sheets. Each star has a data sheet with values from 8 catalogues, and every value is labelled with its catalogue and institution. - DSS2 sky images. Every system has a 5′ DSS2 sky image, loaded only when its panel opens. - Star clusters. The map shows 695 open clusters and moving groups within 500 pc (1,630 ly), taken from Hunt & Reffert 2023. They are plotted at their measured galactic positions. Clicking a cluster shows its distance, member count, age, extinction, radial velocity and proper motion, plus its DSS2 image (119 clusters have one). The Pleiades, Hyades, NGC 3532 and NGC 2547 also have ESO or NOIRLab photos. - Planets. Planet entries now show the discovery method, facility, year and place, plus Teq, density and mass errors. Stars with TESS TOIs list them. - Images. All images are separate JPEGs under img/v5/ and load lazily.

Limitations (please read) - Positional matches only. GALEX, eRASS1 and WDS are matched by position: the Gaia positions are propagated to each survey's epoch, then matched within fixed radii. A few matches will be wrong in crowded fields or close pairs. - eRASS1 sky coverage. eRASS1 covers only the German half of the eROSITA sky (galactic longitude > 180°), so a missing X-ray value does not mean the star is not an X-ray source. - Model-based Gaia values. FLAME ages and masses depend on the models used, and GSP-Phot [M/H] and Teff are known to be biased for M dwarfs. Where catalogues disagree, all values are shown and none are averaged. - Computed values. The W1−W4 colour and the X-ray luminosity are calculated here from catalogue fluxes and the distance. - DSS images. DSS plates date from the 1950s to the 1990s, so nearby high-proper-motion stars (for example alpha Cen) can sit off-centre in their cutouts, and very bright stars saturate. - Missing clusters. The Ursa Major moving group is not in Hunt & Reffert, so it is not shown. Very large, nearby groups such as beta Tuc have their image FOV capped at 12°. - Marker size. Cluster marker size reflects the half-member radius; it is a display choice, not a true angular size. - Sources not added. exoplanet.eu (and its Paris VO TAP) still could not be reached from the build machine on 23 Oct 2026 (TLS errors and timeouts), so it was not added. - LAMOST coverage. LAMOST has a bright limit and observes the northern sky, so only about 90 nearby stars have LAMOST values. LRS M-type matches whose catalogue Gaia DR3 ID disagrees with ours were dropped. LASP [Fe/H] is not calibrated for M dwarfs. - CARMENES matches. These are positional (J2000 positions, 20″ radius, nearest match, one catalogue star per map star) and are shown with their Karmn ID. Wide pairs could be swapped. - JWST pointings. MAST pointings within 36″ of a star are listed. A pointing can belong to a neighbour or a background target, and calibration or target-acquisition exposures are included.

  • Top-10 verification: every finding of the 10 top-ranked systems carries a status: hand-verified (abstract read by hand), abstract-checked (automatic: object+value found in abstract), reference-checked (paper exists, title matches), keyword-derived (not checked), or source-disagrees. v5d additions (2026-10-23)

  • 0.23c routes (3D) for the map's top 10 (P.routes, pipeline/v5/prep_routes.py). Each line runs from the Sun to the nav intercept point (8 systems); for GJ 667 and GJ 628, which are not in the nav v2 set, it runs to the current position and uses the SOURCES_v1.1 trip-time formula. Line colour shows the ship-frame ISM proton energy (γ−1)m_pc², taken from the design speed profile (boost, coast, magsail, fusion). This is a radiation-exposure proxy based on speed only. Each route has a hazard panel listing the LISM clouds (projection only; crossing positions unknown, so not drawn), the assumed n_i = 0.1 cm⁻³, the derived column density, and dust hazard = unknown.
  • Hand verification extended to ranks 11–30 (verify_top10.py): GJ 682, GJ 251, HD 20794, GJ 15 A and GJ 3378 abstracts were read. Badge counts are in the report.
  • Open Exoplanet Catalogue cross-check (prep_oec.py): a substitute for exoplanet.eu, which was unreachable. Flags status conflicts, period differences >2%, mass differences >30% and planets missing from OEC. OEC imports most planets from the NASA archive, so agreement between the two is not independent confirmation.
  • System view: faint blue ellipses show a ± 1σ (NASA pscomppars errors, prep_orberr.py). The caption names every planet whose i, e or ω is unknown and drawn with an ASSUMED value. Ω is assumed to be 0 everywhere.
  • Press images (fetch_press2.py, prep_press2.py): ESA/Hubble, ESA/Webb, ESO and NOIRLab. Each image page was checked for type (Observation/Photographic/Collage), absence of third-party ©, a CC BY 4.0 link, and no artwork or annotated keywords in the title.
  • Accessibility: a colour-blind-safe ramp (cividis routes, blue/yellow HZ), text size (normal / large / x-large) and reduced motion (also honours prefers-reduced-motion). Settings are saved in localStorage.

v6 UI (2026-10-23)

starmap_v6.html uses the same v5 payload and engine (pipeline/v5/template_v5.html) inside a new shell (pipeline/v6/: v6.css, v6_dom.html, v6.js, render_v6.py, shots_v6.js). The changes are all in the interface; no data was added: - the sky fills the screen, with a dock at the bottom; - a ⌘K / / palette searches systems, planets, clusters, routes and commands; - a ranked side list has filter chips and j/k keys; - each system has a tabbed page: Summary, Planets, Findings, Hazards, Images and Data sheet; - breadcrumbs, and Esc to go back; - deep links: #sys=&tab=, #cl=, #route=, #panel=list|settings, #pal=1; - an onboarding tour, saved in localStorage sm6_onboard.

Findings, data-sheet properties and XP light curves (about 5.7 MB) are in starmap_v6_lazy.js. That file loads about 0.6 s after the map appears, so the initial HTML is about 2.5 MB instead of 8 MB.

v6.3 (2026-10-23)

  • Load: starmap_v6.html is now a ~150 KB shell. Data and code live in versioned chunks (?v=<build hash>): starmap_v6_engine.js (three.js + controls), starmap_v6_core.js (positions, clusters, routes, constellations), starmap_v6_lazy.js (findings and data sheets, loaded after first paint) and starmap_v6_sky.js (SIMBAD RA/Dec and proper motions for the Earth sky, loaded only when that view opens). starmap_v6_sw.js is a service worker (http/https only) that precaches the shell and chunks, so repeat visits and offline reloads work. Compression is left to the web host; gzip sizes are reported by render_v6.py. Before/after numbers: perf63_before.json, perf63_after.json (pipeline/v6/perf_v63.js).
  • Sky from Earth (View panel, or #sky=earth&loc=chicago&t=2026-10-23T21:00&ep=2032&lim=suburban): an all-sky dome with horizon, N/E/S/W and 30°/60° altitude circles. Location presets (Chicago by default, America/Chicago), custom latitude/longitude, or “Use my location” (only on click). Date and time are entered in the observer's local time zone. Presets: Now, Tonight 9 pm, and “Pythagoras launch 2032” (same date in 2032; no launch date is in the data). Map stars use SIMBAD (CDS, France) J2000 positions, moved by proper motion and precessed with IAU 1976. Nutation, aberration and refraction are ignored. Background stars: Hipparcos (ESA). Figures: Stellarium. “Visible on this night” lists map stars brighter than the chosen limit that reach ≥15° altitude while the Sun is below −12°. V magnitudes are used where SIMBAD has them, otherwise Gaia G (ESA), which flatters red dwarfs.
  • Label styles: Box (default), Light (half-transparent backdrop plus halo) and Halo (text outline, no box). Choose in the View panel or with #lab=; the choice is remembered. All three pass the AA contrast test over a white glow and over the dark sky.
  • Touch: long-press (~0.5 s) on a star shows the hover card without opening the star.
  • JWST tour is rebuilt from raw/v5/jwobs.json (pipeline/v6/prep_jwobs.py). Of the 277 JWST-tagged literature rows, 24 papers across 14 systems describe new JWST observations in their title or abstract. Their statuses are unchanged: 2 confirmed, 15 reported, 6 non-detection, 1 disputed. Modelling and feasibility papers are excluded, as are earlier “confirmed” JWST rows that were not observations. The tour shows up to 8 systems with quote, citation link, status badge and the MAST positional-match caveat.
